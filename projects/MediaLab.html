Robotic Hand Prosthesis

Biomechatronics Prosthetics
2024

Overview

The challenge was to design an affordable, lightweight prosthetic hand that could provide natural grip control and sensory feedback. Commercial prosthetics are often prohibitively expensive and heavy, limiting accessibility for many users.

This project focused on creating a functional prototype using 3D printing and off-the-shelf components, achieving a 40% weight reduction compared to commercial alternatives while maintaining essential functionality. The design incorporates multiple grip patterns and intuitive control mechanisms.

Key Achievements

  • 40% weight reduction vs. commercial alternatives
  • 5 distinct grip patterns implemented
  • Cost reduction of 60% using 3D printing
  • User testing with 3 participants showing positive feedback

Design Process

1

Research & Requirements

Conducted user interviews and literature review to understand needs. Identified key requirements: weight under 400g, cost under $500, support for common grip patterns, and intuitive control.

2

Concept Development

Explored multiple finger actuation mechanisms: tendon-driven, linkage-based, and direct drive. Selected tendon-driven system for balance of weight, complexity, and force transmission.

3

CAD Design

Designed full assembly in SolidWorks with focus on 3D-printable components. Optimized geometry for strength-to-weight ratio, ensuring all parts could be printed without supports.

4

Prototyping & Testing

Built multiple iterations, testing finger strength, grip patterns, and control algorithms. Refined finger joint design and tendon routing based on test results.

5

Integration & Validation

Integrated EMG sensors and control system. Conducted user testing with 3 participants, collecting feedback on comfort, control intuitiveness, and functionality.

Design Iterations & Tradeoffs

Iteration 1: Initial Prototype

What Changed: Basic finger mechanism with simple tendon routing

Why: Proof of concept for actuation method

Result: Functional but heavy (450g), limited grip patterns

Iteration 2: Weight Reduction

What Changed: Hollow finger design, optimized joint geometry, lighter servos

Why: User feedback indicated weight was primary concern

Tradeoff: Slightly reduced grip force (15N vs 20N)

Result: 40% weight reduction (270g), acceptable force for daily tasks

Iteration 3: Control System

What Changed: Improved EMG signal processing, added grip pattern selection

Why: Users needed more intuitive control and multiple grips

Tradeoff: Increased complexity and power consumption

Result: 5 distinct grip patterns, 0.5s response time, better user satisfaction
