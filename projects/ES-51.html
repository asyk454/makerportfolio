Two-Speed 6WD Robot with 4-Bar Extending Claw

Class ES 51: Computer-Aided Machine Design
Timeline Jan — Apr 2025
Skills Learned
  • Mechanical Design
  • Lathing, Milling
  • Silicone Molding
  • Team Management

Summary

As part of a team of six, I designed, manufactured, and assembled a field-competition robot balancing maneuverability, scoring flexibility, and robustness under strict time, material, and actuator constraints. The robot featured a two-speed gear-shifting 6WD drivetrain, a four-bar arm, and a servo-driven silicone claw.

Context

A team-based mechanical design competition (ES 51: Turf Wars) involving 4-minute head to head robot matches on a turf field with 30° ramps, strict hardware constraints, and diverse game objects.

Design Goal:

We aimed to build a robot that balanced:

  • Quick maneuverability
  • Ramp-climbing torque
  • In-game strategy adaptability
  • High scoring capability

My Role

I served as the team lead/organizer, owning these roles:

  • Managed team project timeline accountability
  • Authored match strategy design
  • Manufactured wheels and axels along with mechanical assembly
  • Significantly contributed to mechanical assembly

While calculations, CAD design, and other manufacturing elements were shared across the team, I focused on coordinating design decisions and ensuring the robot could be built, repaired, and deployed on time.

Design Prototyping and Selection

Drove early-stage prototyping and design selection (sketches to cardboard builds) culminating in a two-speed 6WD drivetrain and four-bar telescoping arm.

Implementation / Build Evidence

Fabricated laser-cut acrylic chassis plates, 3D printed mounts and gears, silicone-cast wheels and grippers, and lathe-turned shafts for precise fits.

Testing & Results

We assembled the robot under deadline pressure and had limited integration testing. The first match failed due to tolerance and assembly issues, while the second match succeeded defensively after quick fixes. The drivetrain remained reliable after a gearbox redesign that reduced part count.

Reflection & Improvements

  • Schedule full-system testing earlier to catch tolerance stackups.
  • Improve arm tolerancing and QC checks before competition day.
  • Allocate time for tuning the shifter so it delivers real match value.
