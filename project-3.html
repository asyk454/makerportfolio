Biomechatronic Device

Biomechatronics Research
2024

Overview

Developed a research device for studying human-machine interfaces with real-time motion tracking and feedback systems. This project was part of research at the MIT Media Lab Biomechatronics group, focusing on understanding how humans interact with assistive devices.

The device integrates multiple sensors including IMUs, force sensors, and EMG electrodes to capture comprehensive motion and muscle activity data. The system achieved 95% accuracy in motion tracking compared to gold-standard motion capture systems.

Key Achievements

  • 95% accuracy in motion tracking
  • Real-time data acquisition at 100Hz
  • Multi-modal sensor fusion
  • Used in 3 published research studies

Research Studies

Design Process

1

Requirements Analysis

Defined sensor requirements based on research objectives. Identified need for multi-modal sensing: motion (IMU), force (load cells), and muscle activity (EMG).

2

Mechanical Design

Designed wearable device housing with proper sensor placement. Ensured minimal interference with natural movement while maintaining sensor contact.

3

Sensor Integration

Integrated multiple sensor types with synchronized data acquisition. Developed calibration procedures for accurate measurements.

4

Validation & Testing

Validated system against gold-standard motion capture. Conducted user studies to verify accuracy and usability in real-world conditions.

Design Iterations & Tradeoffs

Iteration 1: Single Sensor Type

What Changed: Initial prototype with IMU only

Why: Proof of concept for motion tracking

Result: 75% accuracy, insufficient for research needs

Iteration 2: Multi-Modal Sensing

What Changed: Added force sensors and EMG electrodes

Why: Need for comprehensive biomechanical data

Tradeoff: Increased complexity and power consumption

Result: 95% accuracy, suitable for research applications
